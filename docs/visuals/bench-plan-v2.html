Yes, this is buildable with what you have. The pod and all the demo-lab parts are already in your hands. You still need: one breadboard from the store, a way to hold a phone over the desk, and possibly an NPN transistor for the optional 4th lab. You don't need anything else.
Pico + ADS1115 chip → your Arduino Uno. It can already measure 0–5 V on 6 pins, so no extra chip is needed.
Logitech webcam (out of stock) → a teammate's phone on a DIY stand.
Separate ground clip → the pod powers the lab board, which keeps everything at a safe 5 V and shares ground automatically.
Printed corner markers → tape the board down and click its 4 corners once. Markers become optional.

The setup at a glance

Six things on the desk. The table below says what each one does, using a human TA as the comparison.

1Phone camera
= Bench's eyes
Held about 25 cm above the lab board, pointing straight down. It streams video to the laptop so Bench can see where parts are and draw "look here" highlights on the screen. It doesn't measure anything.
Made from: a teammate's phone + a DIY stand
2Lab breadboard
= the student's workbench
The circuit the student builds from the lab manual. It's taped down so the camera's view of it never shifts, which keeps the screen highlights lined up.
Made from: 830-pt breadboard (store) + starter-kit parts
3Probe pod
= a multimeter Bench can read for itself
An Arduino that measures the voltage on 4 wires about 20 times a second and sends the numbers to the laptop. It also has the talk button and a status light.
Made from: Uno R3 + kit breadboard, in one 3D-printed dock
4Probe wires
= the multimeter's tips
Four colored wires. When Bench says "put the yellow wire in row 10," the student plugs it in there and the pod reads that row's voltage.
Made from: kit jumper wires, each through a 10 kΩ safety resistor
5Power wires
= the bench power supply
A red and a black wire carry the pod's 5 V and ground to the lab board's rails. The circuit can't go above 5 V, and the pod always shares the circuit's ground, so its readings are correct.
Made from: 2 jumper wires
6Laptop
= Bench's brain, voice and whiteboard
Runs all the software. It takes in the camera feed and probe numbers, asks Claude what to do next, speaks through Cartesia, and shows the live feed with highlights, the manual and the readings.
Made from: your laptop (mic + speaker built in)

How the probe pod is wired

Everything plugs into the kit breadboard next to the Uno. No soldering.

Demo labs you can build from the kit

Two core labs make a full demo. Each has faults you can set up in seconds, or that a judge can introduce.

Lab 1 · LED + resistor Core
Parts
Red LED, 220 Ω, 2 wires
Faults to set up
  • LED backwards: probe reads 5.0 V instead of ~2 V, so no current is flowing
  • 10 kΩ instead of 220 Ω: the LED is very dim, and its voltage is lower than expected
  • LED leg one row off: the row reads 5 V and the LED is dark
  • Missing ground wire: every point reads 5 V
Lab 2 · Light sensor Core
Parts
Photoresistor, 10 kΩ, 3 wires
Faults to set up
  • 220 Ω instead of 10 kΩ: the output barely moves when the light changes
  • Output wire on the wrong row: it reads a fixed 0 or 5 V
  • Live moment: the student covers the sensor and the on-screen gauge moves. Bench asks, "why did it go up?"
Lab 3 · IR receiver Stretch
Parts
IR receiver, remote, LED + 220 Ω
Why it matters
It's the closest match to the ENGR 10 IR board Aadi TAs. The output sits near 5 V and flickers low when the remote is pressed, and the pod can see that flicker.
Faults to set up
  • Output pin not connected, or a missing 5 V wire. Never stage reversed power, because it can kill the sensor.
Lab 4 · Transistor switch Stretch
Parts
NPN transistor (store), button, 1 kΩ, LED + 220 Ω
Faults to set up
  • Transistor turned around: the base reads ~0.7 V but the LED stays off
  • Button wired to the wrong row: the base never rises when the button is pressed

The one 3D print: a "pod dock"

A flat tray that holds the Uno and the pod breadboard together, with low walls and raised Y / B / G / W labels where the probe wires come out. It's polish, not something the demo depends on. If the print fails, use cardboard and tape.
