The bench, seen from the side

The probe pod is a small box of leads. The student plugs a lead into the breadboard row Bench asks for, and Bench reads the real voltage there instead of guessing from the camera.

1Phone on a gooseneck clamp, looking straight down (Continuity Camera)
2Breadboard with 4 printed ArUco tags at its corners so the software knows where every hole is
3Probe pod: Pico + ADC in a printed box, a big push-to-talk button, and 4 colored leads
4Yellow probe plugged into row 10, the row Bench highlighted on screen
5Black ground lead, clipped once to the circuit's ground rail at setup
6One USB cable: power + readings ~20×/s to the laptop
7Laptop runs everything and shows the live feed, manual and probe gauges

Inside the pod

Everything sits on a half-size breadboard inside the box, using parts with headers already soldered. No soldering.

4 probe leadsMale jumper wires, colored yellow / blue / green / white, + 1 black ground

Protection + divider100k / 33k resistors per lead. Scales 0–10 V down to 0–2.5 V and limits current
→
ADS1115 ADC16-bit, 4 channels, talks I²C. The part you write your own C driver for
→
Raspberry Pi PicoYour C firmware: samples, averages, reads the button
